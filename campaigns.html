Storytelling Nostalgia

Paper Boat — "Memories in a bottle"

Paper Boat doesn't really sell juice. It sells a version of childhood that people are happy to buy back. Every bottle, name and line of copy points at a memory rather than a flavour.

What I notice
The product is the prop. The memory is the product.
Why it works
Nostalgia is the cheapest emotion to trigger and the hardest to fake. Consistency is what makes it believable.
What I'd steal
Pick one emotional territory and never wander off it — then let packaging and story do the selling.
Cover image Amul Placeholder — drop an image here
Agility Illustration

Amul — the topical butter girl

Amul reacts to culture faster than most newsrooms. The format never changes, which is exactly why the comment always lands — you recognise it before you've read a word.

What I notice
A fixed visual system is what buys the freedom to be spontaneous.
Why it works
Speed plus familiarity turns a brand into a companion to the conversation, not an interruption.
What I'd steal
Own a repeatable format, then spend your energy on being timely rather than being clever.
Cover image Nike Placeholder — drop an image here
Conviction Risk

Nike — "Dream Crazy"

A brand deciding what it stands for, out loud, knowing it will lose some customers. The line that made it work wasn't clever — it was committed.

What I notice
The ad is less about the athlete and more about the person watching it.
Why it works
Conviction creates belonging. People don't rally around products; they rally around positions.
What I'd steal
Say the thing you actually believe, and design for the people who'll agree.
Cover image Fevicol Placeholder — drop an image here
Humour Recall

Fevicol — the ad everyone remembers

An industrial adhesive is not a fun product. Fevicol made it human, funny and memorable — and turned a category no one thinks about into a brand everyone quotes.

What I notice
The joke always demonstrates the product truth: it holds.
Why it works
Entertainment earns attention; the product benefit is the punchline, so recall sticks.
What I'd steal
Find the one true thing about the product and turn it into the joke — never the other way round.
