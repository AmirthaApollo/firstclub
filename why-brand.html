Why brand marketing

Five reasons I care about brands.

This is the part of marketing I keep circling back to: why a brand earns a place in someone's life, and what it takes to keep it.

I love understanding why people choose one brand over another.

Most purchase decisions are not really about the product. They're about what someone believes the choice says about them, what they've been told to trust, and how a brand makes them feel in the ten seconds before they reach for it. That's the part I find genuinely interesting.

It shows up in tiny things: shelf position, the word on a label, the colour of a cap, the way a return policy feels. None of it is loud. All of it decides something.

I love good writing.

A headline is a compressed argument. If you can say something complicated in six words, you understand it, and you can probably make other people care about it too. I rewrite sentences for fun. I keep phrases I like in a notes app the way other people keep recipes.

Brand voice is where writing meets consistency. One good line is a happy accident; the same voice for three years is a decision.

I love campaigns that make people feel something.

The ones people quote, remember, or send to a friend. They don't always look expensive, they just land. Big ideas, a sharp insight, and the discipline to say one thing instead of five. When it works, the campaign stops feeling like advertising and starts feeling like culture.

That's the bar I'm chasing: not "did we run a campaign", but "will anyone remember it next month".

I enjoy the thinking behind the execution.

The unglamorous middle is my favourite part: finding the insight, writing the brief, arguing about the idea, getting the right people aligned, then watching what the numbers say afterwards. Ideas are cheap until someone has to ship one.

I like the coordination as much as the concept. Deadlines, approvals and a dozen details are where good campaigns are actually made or lost.

I like having opinions about brands.

Why some brands become shorthand in real life, why a brilliant ad can still feel forced, why one competitor is trusted and another is merely known. A logo can make you recognisable. It can't make you chosen.

I'll happily spend a dinner explaining why a campaign worked. That probably tells you more about how I'd approach the role than any list of skills.

What this means for the role

From interest to the actual job.

Brand marketing is one of the few jobs where all of the above is the job. Consumer insights are just organised curiosity. A brief is good writing with a deadline. A 360-degree campaign is a single idea that has to survive every channel it touches.

I'd bring a bias for the whole arc: understand the consumer, write the thought down clearly, help the idea travel, then look honestly at what happened. Brand consistency isn't a constraint to me; it's the thing that makes everything else easier.

Why FirstClub

The most interesting brief in the room.

Most Indian retail marketing defaults to one promise: cheaper than the other guy. FirstClub seems to be testing a harder, better idea: that people will choose quality and trust when it's made feel accessible, not aspirational.

That's a brand-building problem, not a discount problem. It needs a voice, a point of view, and campaigns that make better everyday choices feel like the sensible modern default rather than a treat. That's exactly the kind of problem I'd want to work on.
